Project Overview

As part of MIT's 16.522 Space Propulsion course, our team designed, built, and tested a Hall thruster.

Often used on small satellites, Hall thrusters are highly efficient ion engines that use electric and magnetic fields to accelerate ionized propellant (usually xenon) to high velocities, producing thrust for spacecraft attitude control and orbital maneuvering.

My Contributions

As the Electronics and Testing Subteam Lead, I was responsible for:

  • Designing and implementing the power supply and control electronics for the thruster
  • Design and integration (into test stands) of diagnostic tools: Current Collector (CC) and Retarding Potential Analyser (RPA)
  • Developing a DAQ system to collect current and voltage readings
  • Coordinating vacuum chamber testing in MIT's AstroVac facility
  • Collecting and analyzing performance data (thrust, specific impulse, efficiency)
  • Troubleshooting electrical and operational issues during testing
  • Contributing to the final technical report and presentation

Team Technical Approach

Design Phase:

  • Studied Hall thruster physics: plasma dynamics and magnetic field configurations
  • Designed thruster geometry to optimize ion acceleration
  • Chose appropriate materials for thruster and electrodes
  • Sized magnetic coils for required field strength

Testing Phase:

  • Prepared the thruster for vacuum chamber testing
  • Conducted firing tests to get stable plasma flow
  • Varied operating parameters (voltage, flow rate, magnetic field) to characterize performance
  • Captured images and videos of plasma behavior
  • Measured diagnostics using a Current Collector and a Retarding Potential Analyzer (RPA), set up on a dedicated test stand

Current Collector

The Current Collector measures the total current leaving the thruster, allowing us to calculate anode efficiency and mass flow rate. The system assumes 100% plume capture and accounts for 81.5% mesh transparency. Manufactured primarily from waterjet-cut aluminum, the CC uses o-ring terminals for electrical connections, secured with lock nuts to keep good contact during testing.

Retarding Potential Analyzer (RPA)

The RPA enables measurement of ion energy distribution, to assess energy efficiency and specific impulse. With 82% mesh transparency, the RPA is also made of waterjet-machined aluminum with O-ring terminal electrical connections secured by lock nuts. Both the CC and RPA were electrically isolated using Kapton tape to prevent interference and ensure accuracy.

Results & Performance

Successfully achieved stable Hall thruster operation in the vacuum chamber. Key performance metrics:

  • Generated visible plasma discharge with characteristic annular plume shape
  • Analyzed thruster efficiency and specific impulse
  • Identified optimal conditions for different voltage/current parameters
  • Documented instabilities we encountered in the hall thruster output and proposed future strategies to address them
