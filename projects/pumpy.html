The Problem

Ocean monitoring is critical for climate research and ecosystem studies, but current UUVs face some limitations:

  • Limited Battery Life: Li-ion batteries support only 3 days at 3 knots
  • Insufficient Range: can't support multi-month ocean monitoring missions
  • High Costs: surface ships cost ~$70,000/day to monitor UUVs
  • Glider Energy Drain: buoyancy changes consume significant power

Our Solution: PUMPY

PUMPY is a cost-effective buoyancy engine that runs on aluminum fuel, providing an alternative to battery dependent systems. The engine generates a chemical reaction between aluminum fuel and an ammonia solution to produce gases that adjust the buoyancy of a UUV. The engine provides 42x greater energy density than Li-Ion batteries, enabling unmanned underwater vehicles (UUVs) to operate for months at depths up to 500m while reducing battery consumption by 90%.

Chemical Reaction

The core of PUMPY is the exothermic reaction between aluminum and water:

2 Al(s) + 6 H₂O → 3 H₂(g) + 2 Al(OH)₃(aq) + Q

Buoyancy Control

  • Ascent: aluminum fuel and ammonia are pumped into a reaction bladder, generating hydrogen gas and heat. The engine's bladder is inflated, increasing buoyancy and causing the UUV to ascend
  • Descent: gases are vented from bladders through a check valve system, decreasing buoyancy and allowing the UUV to descend
  • Neutral Buoyancy: gas volume is fine tuned to achieve neutral buoyancy for stationary monitoring at specific depths
  • Moment Balance: pitch angle is adjusted by managing gas distribution throughout the engine's bladders

System Components

  • Fuel Storage: aluminum paste and the ammonia solution are stored in flexible bladders
  • Pumping System: peristaltic pumps allow precise reactant dispensing with PWM control
  • Reaction Chamber: Y-joint mixing system to combine reactants
  • Gas Bladders: silicone rubber bladders (350mL capacity) that inflate/deflate to control buoyancy
  • Check Valve System: 2 psi cracking pressure valve for controlled gas venting
  • Electronics & Controls: ESP32 microcontroller with custom PCB for pump actuation and sensor monitoring
  • Enclosures: waterproof housing for electronics and flooded hull for reaction components

PUMPY -> CALIPSO

Following the initial PUMPY design, the team developed CALIPSO (a descoped proof-of-concept configuration)

Descoping Changes:

  • Reduced operational depth from 500m to 5m for testing
  • Substituted distilled water for the ammonia solution to simplify handling
  • Removed fine buoyancy control and moment balance features
  • Split system into two independent enclosures for easier assembly
  • Focused on proving fundamental buoyancy change capability

CALIPSO Test Results:

  • Successfully demonstrated aluminum fuel reaction can generate buoyancy change underwater
  • Validated complete dive cycle: hull flooding → gas generation → bladder inflation → gas venting → descent
  • Achieved reaction temperatures up to 80°C and peak overpressure of 6 psi
  • Demonstrated successful bladder venting and hull reflooding for multiple cycles
