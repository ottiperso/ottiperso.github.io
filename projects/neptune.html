Communication Like Submarines for Scuba Divers

The Problem

Underwater communication is dangerous. Divers rely on hand signals, which only work when they're looking at each other. In an emergency, that's often too late.

Critical situations happen fast:

  • Running out of air
  • Equipment malfunction
  • Medical emergencies
  • Disorientation in murky water

The consequence? Panicked divers make rapid ascents to the surface, risking decompression sickness, which is potentially fatal.

Hand signals fail when divers aren't looking, in low visibility, or when instructors need to monitor entire groups.

Our Solution: NepΨune

NepΨune is an underwater communication device that allows scuba divers to send and receive pre-programmed messages using ultrasonic sonar technology. Inspired by submarine communication systems, NepΨune enables divers to communicate instantly and clearly, even in low visibility.

How It Works...

The Three-Piece System

  • →
    Earpiece: Bone-conducting earpiece that comfortably latches onto any dive mask, remaining aligned to the skull throughout the dive
  • →
    Electronics Housing: Waterproof enclosure containing the circuit board, amplifiers, mode switches, hydrophone, and battery (rated to 500 kPa of pressure)
  • →
    Button Interface: Six large buttons corresponding to the most commonly used hand signals

Six Pre-Programmed Messages:

ASCEND
MARINE LIFE
LOOK AT ME
SOMETHING'S OFF
AIR
SOS

When a diver realizes they're running out of air, they can push the AIR button on NepΨune's interface. Through their earpiece, a confirmation is played back. Others immediately hear: "User 1 says AIR" and can respond to help.

The Technology: Submarine-Grade Sonar

With NepΨune, scuba divers can communicate like submarines.

Hydrophones & Ultrasonic Sonar Waves

  • Hydrophones act as both underwater microphones and speakers
  • Using sonar waves, each device sends and receives a set of morse-code-like sequences in the ultrasonic frequency range
  • NepΨune's encoding and decoding algorithms make sure that messages are packaged and received in a way that minimizes noise interference

Circuit Logic

  • The circuit converts button presses (alerts) to a binary message that corresponds to audio other users can hear in their headset
  • A system like this would normally use two hydrophones (one for sending, one for receiving), but our mode-selecting switches and mode-specific amplifiers need only one hydrophone

Bone Conduction Audio

  • The bone-conducting earpiece uses vibrations to turn the skull into a speaker, so users hear alerts clearly underwater

Engineering Specifications

  • Pressure Rating: 500 kPa at a depth of 50 meters
  • Waterproof Housings: Each component is encased in corrosion resistant waterproof housings
  • Pressure-Rated Buttons: Buttons are also rated to 50m depth (water pressure won't trigger them automatically)
  • Battery Life: 6-hour battery life (supports a full day of diving)
  • Wireless Charging: Devices are recharged wirelessly
  • Universal Compatibility: Earpiece works with any dive mask
