Project Overview

Selected as 1 of 12 MIT post-graduate students for the prestigious MIT Portugal Material Impact on Sound Attenuation held in the Azores. This intensive program combined theoretical lectures with hands-on fieldwork, focusing on marine robotics, oceanography, and ecosystem monitoring.

The program brought together students, professors, field experts, and the local community to address real-world environmental challenges through robotics and technology.

The Challenge

The Azores archipelago faces a growing problem with invasive algae species that threaten local marine ecosystems. Traditional monitoring methods are labor-intensive and provide limited coverage of affected areas.

Our team was tasked with designing and operating a Remotely Operated Vehicle (ROV) capable of tracking and documenting invasive algae populations around Faial Island, providing crucial data for conservation efforts.

My Approach & Contributions

  • Collaborated with an international team of engineers and scientists to design ROV specifications based on mission requirements
  • Participated in hands-on assembly and testing of the ROV system
  • Conducted field operations in ocean conditions, piloting and monitoring the ROV
  • Worked with marine biologists to identify and document invasive algae species
  • Analyzed collected data and contributed to final project presentations
  • Engaged with local community stakeholders to understand environmental impact and conservation goals

Results & Impact

Successfully deployed and operated the ROV in multiple locations around Faial Island, collecting valuable data on invasive algae distribution. The project demonstrated the feasibility of using affordable, custom-built ROV systems for environmental monitoring in remote locations.

The data collected will support ongoing conservation efforts and inform local policy decisions regarding marine ecosystem management.

Technical Details

ROV Capabilities:

  • Depth rating suitable for coastal algae monitoring
  • HD camera system for underwater documentation
  • Real-time video feedback to surface operators
  • Stable control system for precise maneuvering in ocean currents
  • Integration with data logging systems for scientific analysis
